4. Phase A — Step-by-Step in Microsoft Excel (220k-Row Audit)

Step A1 — Load via Excel Data Model PivotTable

Use Data → From Text/CSV → Load To… → PivotTable Report (Add to Data Model) on datasets/20-talentfirst-hr/data/hr_workforce.csv. Put OverTime in Rows and Attrition in Columns (% of Row Total): verify OverTime = Yes → 33.71% Attrition vs. No → 17.58%!
💡 Why do we do this?

Reason: The CHRO's brief opens with a scary bill — $2.41B of turnover cost — but before costing anything you must validate the DRIVERS, and OverTime is the prime suspect. The hand-built cross-tab with % of Row Total converts raw counts into comparable quit rates per group (the Set 17 discipline: rates within groups, never counts across groups). It's the cheapest possible root-cause test, and its result becomes the app's headline statistic.

Real-time example: The pivot delivers the shock number instantly: employees working overtime quit at 33.71% — nearly DOUBLE the 17.58% baseline of those who don't. And the exposure is big: 48,295 employees (21.95% of staff) carry OverTime. In one pivot you've found the largest CONTROLLABLE attrition lever in the company — overtime policy — which becomes Page 1's shock bar, Page 2's toggle, and the justification for the entire burnout intervention program.

What happens if you skip it: The app ships as a headcount scorecard ("we lose 21% of staff") with no lever — the CHRO already knows the aggregate; what she needs is the driver she can act on.

Smart-work tip: In people analytics, hand-test every hypothesized driver (overtime, satisfaction, promotion wait, tenure) as a simple 2×2 cross-tab before building anything — the ones that survive become the app's visuals, and you'll know the story before you open Power BI.

5. Phase B — Complete 3-Page Power BI People-Analytics Build

Stage 1 & 2 — Power Query Banding & Flight-Risk Flagging

  1. Import data/hr_workforce.csv → Transform Data → rename to Workforce.
  2. Set HireDate and TerminationDate = Date (blank TerminationDate becomes null cleanly for the 173,526 active staff!). Set MonthlyIncome & AnnualSalary = Currency ($), and survey/tenure fields = Whole Number.
  3. Add ordered TenureBand (0–1 yr, 2–4 yrs, 5–9 yrs, 10+ yrs) + TenureSort (1..4).
  4. Add Custom Column FlightRiskTier for Active Employees: 
    if [Attrition] = "Yes" then "Departed"
else if [OverTime] = "Yes" and [JobSatisfaction] = 1 and [WorkLifeBalance] = 1 then "1 - Critical Red Alert"
else if [OverTime] = "Yes" and ([JobSatisfaction] = 1 or [WorkLifeBalance] = 1 or [YearsSinceLastPromotion] >= 5) then "2 - High Risk"
else "3 - Normal"
  5. Click Home → Close & Apply.
💡 Why do we do this?

Reason: This stage converts an HRIS export into an action system. The TenureBand orders the attrition curve so the onboarding-dip pattern can be read (Set 15's banding, third appearance). The FlightRiskTier custom column is the capstone's most consequential move: it encodes the CHRO's triage POLICY as DATA — every employee gets a tier derived from their burnout profile. Building it in Power Query (not DAX) makes the tier a real column: slicable, RLS-filterable, and exportable to the roster.

Real-time example: Read the tier logic as hospital triage: Red Alert = active + overtime + BOTH survey scores at the minimum — the triple-burnout cohort that historically quits at 50.27% (565 people have ever fit it; 281 still work here). High Risk = overtime plus ANY single red flag, including 5+ years since promotion (a 135,269-person stagnation pool worth monitoring at 23.30% attrition). The tier column is literally the CHRO's intuition written in M — auditable, consistent, and applied identically to all 220,000 employees.

What happens if you skip it: HR Business Partners receive a 220,000-row export and must ask "who do I call first?" — retention effort spreads evenly over everyone instead of concentrating on the 281 imminent resignations where it actually pays.

Smart-work tip: The moment a stakeholder describes a manual triage rule ("I'd worry about people with X and Y"), encode it as a tier column immediately — turning expert intuition into a filterable, consistent artifact is the core skill of people analytics.

Stage 3 & 4 — People-Analytics & Financial ROI DAX + What-If Parameter

Create Numeric Range What-If Parameter Replacement Cost % (`Min 0.30`, `Max 1.00`, `Increment 0.10`, `Default 0.50`). Then write:

Total Workforce = COUNTROWS ( 'Workforce' )
// Check: 220,000

Active Headcount =
CALCULATE ( [Total Workforce], 'Workforce'[Attrition] = "No" )
// Check: 173,526

Departed Headcount =
CALCULATE ( [Total Workforce], 'Workforce'[Attrition] = "Yes" )
// Check: 46,474

Attrition Rate % = DIVIDE ( [Departed Headcount], [Total Workforce] )
// Check: 21.12%

OverTime Share % =
DIVIDE (
    CALCULATE ( [Total Workforce], 'Workforce'[OverTime] = "Yes" ),
    [Total Workforce]
)
// Check: 21.95%

Avg Annual Salary = AVERAGE ( 'Workforce'[AnnualSalary] )
// Check: $104,371

Estimated Turnover Cost =
VAR Mult = 'Replacement Cost %'[Replacement Cost % Value]
VAR DepartedPayroll =
    CALCULATE ( SUM ( 'Workforce'[AnnualSalary] ), 'Workforce'[Attrition] = "Yes" )
RETURN
    DepartedPayroll * Mult
// Check at default 50% (0.50): $2,413,178,590 ($2.41B!)

Critical Flight-Risk Active =
CALCULATE (
    [Total Workforce],
    'Workforce'[FlightRiskTier] = "1 - Critical Red Alert"
)
// Check: Exactly 281 active employees!
💡 Why do we do this?

Reason: The headcount measures are simple filters, but the centerpiece is Estimated Turnover Cost — and it teaches a money lesson HR analytics specifically needs: replacement cost (recruiting fees, lost productivity, ramp-up time) is genuinely UNKNOWABLE per employee — industry convention estimates 30–100% of annual salary. Instead of pretending precision, the measure multiplies departed payroll ($4.83B) by a user-set What-If multiplier: the assumption becomes an interactive slider the CFO can defend.

Real-time example: At the default 50%, the card reads $2.41B ($2,413,178,590) — a number bigger than most product lines' revenue, which is exactly why the retention program gets funded. Slide to 30% for the conservative case (~$1.45B) or 100% for worst case ($4.83B): the board now debates the MULTIPLIER instead of disputing the model. Notice also what the Critical Flight-Risk Active check does: counting exactly 281 validates the tier logic against the guide's verified roster — a reconciliation check, in people-analytics form.

What happens if you skip it: Turnover cost appears either as an ignored footnote or as one fabricated number nobody trusts — and the 281-person roster can't be tracked as a KPI without its measure.

Smart-work tip: When a key input is inherently uncertain, surface it as a What-If parameter with a defensible range (here the 30–100% replacement-cost convention) — uncertainty made interactive is more credible than false precision, and it invites the CFO into the model instead of against it.

Page 2 Burnout Heatmap Matrix & DEI Compensation Equity Lab

1. Heatmap Matrix: Rows = JobSatisfaction (1..4), Columns = WorkLifeBalance (1..4), Values = [Attrition Rate %] (enable Background color gradient!), plus an OverTime toggle button — watch cell (Sat=1, WLB=1) glow bright crimson at 50.27% attrition when OverTime = Yes!
2. DEI Pay-Equity Clustered Bar: Y = Department, X = [Avg Annual Salary], Legend = Gender (`Female $104,541` · `Non-binary $104,323` · `Male $104,250`) — verifying compensation parity across all 8 departments!

💡 Why do we do this?

Reason: The heatmap is a burnout INTERACTION detector: satisfaction × work-life-balance on the grid, attrition % as color, and the OverTime toggle turning it into a 2×2×2 experiment. Attrition drivers rarely act alone — the most alarming number in this dataset is a COMBINATION, not a single factor. The DEI bar is the audit half of the page: pay parity per department, deliberately placed beside burnout because compensation equity and workload equity are both retention levers, and CHRO reviews always cover both.

Real-time example: Use the toggle as the demo moment: with OverTime = No, the worst heatmap cell is elevated but survivable. Flick OverTime = Yes and cell (Sat=1, WLB=1) burns crimson at 50.27% — HALF of that cohort quits. That's proof overtime AMPLIFIES low satisfaction and low WLB into resignations far beyond what any factor does alone (33.71% OT, 29.95% Sat=1, 28.62% WLB=1 individually). Meanwhile the DEI bar reads Female $104,541 · Non-binary $104,323 · Male $104,250 — near-perfect parity (~0.3% spread) the CHRO can now SHOW the board and auditors rather than merely assert.

What happens if you skip it: Burnout stays three disconnected KPIs; the interaction effect — the single most alarming statistic available — never appears anywhere, and the DEI position stays an unsupported claim in a compliance document.

Smart-work tip: Heatmaps with a categorical toggle are a poor man's interaction analysis. Always color-scale the RATE (never headcount) so tiny groups can't fake danger. And pair audits with risk analyses on one page: audits prove fairness, risk analyses demand action — CHRO pages need both.

Page 3 Proactive HRBP Flight-Risk Roster (281 Critical Employees) + RLS

1. Filter Page 3 to Attrition = "No" and add a Slicer on FlightRiskTier defaulted to 1 - Critical Red Alert (281 employees!). Display EmployeeID, Name, Department, JobRole, OfficeLocation, AnnualSalary, YearsSinceLastPromotion, and TrainingHours so HR Business Partners can schedule immediate stay-interviews and workload relief!
2. Configure Row-Level Security (RLS) for Privacy: Click Modeling → Manage roles → create role IndiaHRBP with DAX filter 'Workforce'[OfficeLocation] IN {"Mumbai", "Bengaluru", "Delhi", "Hyderabad"} so regional HR managers only view PII in their assigned country!

💡 Why do we do this?

Reason: The roster is where people analytics crosses its finish line: prediction converted into a named, schedulable list. The 281 red-alert employees surface with department, promotion wait, salary, and training hours — everything an HRBP needs for a stay-interview. And RLS is non-negotiable in HR: employee PII and compensation data must be visible ONLY to the regional owners accountable for acting on it — a privacy requirement, not a feature.

Real-time example: Watch the loop close in Bengaluru: the IndiaHRBP role filters to Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, Hyderabad, so that HRBP sees their regional slice of the 281 red-alert employees (4 of the 10 global hubs are Indian, so a large share of the roster) and schedules stay-interviews and workload relief THIS WEEK — while being structurally unable to see Tokyo's salaries, and vice versa. Then the What-If retention-bonus calculator completes the business case: the cost of intervening on 281 people is trivially small against the $2.41B turnover baseline — saving even a quarter of the cohort pays back multiples.

What happens if you skip it: Analytics stops at a diagnosis deck; the 281 resign over the coming quarters and the $2.41B lands on next year's P&L. And publishing unsecured employee-level salary data is an instant privacy incident (Mistake 2) — in HR, that single mistake can terminate the entire analytics program.

Smart-work tip: Every predictive people-analytics project should end with an RLS-protected roster, a named owner, and a scheduled action — a risk model that produces no worklist is a research paper, not an app. Configure RLS BEFORE publishing, always test with View as, and remember the pattern (Set 18's stretch, now mission-critical).

8. Trainer's Troubleshooting: Common Beginner Mistakes

Mistake 1 — Deleting rows where TerminationDate is blank: Blank TerminationDate means the employee STILL WORKS HERE (`173,526` active staff)! Deleting blanks makes Attrition look like 100%!
